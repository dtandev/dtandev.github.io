RISPECT (Regional Innovation Scheme Project for Exploration Capabilities and Technologies) is a
research and innovation project awarded by EIT RawMaterials under Horizon Europe, in its
programme for regional innovation. It runs from February 2025 to the end of 2026 and aims to
introduce and validate technologies for exploring critical raw materials, such as lithium,
bauxite, copper and nickel, in regions of Poland, Greece, Albania and Ukraine, so that
exploration costs less and puts less strain on the environment.

<!--more-->

Three technologies come together. TerraEye leads the work on synthetic hyperspectral imagery
generated from multispectral satellite data, WIDMO from Poland provides a ground-penetrating
radar system, and SAISLAB from Greece an AI platform that brings the data of both together.

**What we want to achieve.** A hyperspectral sensor records hundreds of narrow bands, so every
mineral shows up with its own spectral signature, but such data are scarce and cover small
areas. Multispectral satellites such as Sentinel-2 and ASTER have only a few broad bands, but
they cover the whole world. We are not trying to reproduce the hyperspectral signature itself.
What we want to reproduce are the analytical capabilities that come from it, such as
recognising minerals and groups of minerals, and to do that by fusing the data of the
multispectral sensors.

**What makes it difficult.** The sensors have different spatial resolutions, from 10 to 30
metres, and their data come from different periods: Sentinel-2 is a current source, while the
shortwave infrared archive of ASTER is historical. The data cannot simply be stacked on top of
each other. The method has to combine the complementary information of both sensors in one
common representation, to say how certain its answer is, and to be clear about what cannot be
recovered. The result is a spectral representation aligned with hyperspectral data, not a claim
to recover information that is missing from the input.
